[CAMERA — EDITION A]

My Project 1 decision is whether
a buy-side investment committee
with no current Cadence position
should initiate,
watch and defer,
or not initiate.

My Edition A view was simple.

Cadence looked strong enough
to pass my screens,
so I expected the valuation work
to support an investment.

That was a research thesis—
not a completed recommendation.

//

[SHOW EDITION B]

The completed work changed my view
to WATCH AND DEFER,
with no position,
at the September first market price
of three hundred thirteen dollars
and four cents.

The change came from
a mixture of course instruction,
my own analysis,
peer challenge,
and disclosed AI assistance.

The integrated F-C-F-F D-C-F
produced a range of
fifty-seven dollars and twenty cents
to one hundred eleven dollars
and fifty-two cents,
with a base value
of eighty-three dollars
and thirty-four cents.

Synopsys produced
higher reference values:

one hundred eighty-nine dollars
and sixty-four cents
from E-V to revenue,

and two hundred nine dollars
and forty-seven cents
from P-to-E.

But both were still
below the market.

The methods disagreed
on the amount of overvaluation—
not on the action.

//

[SHOW WACC VALIDATION]

One material AI-assisted output
was the revised ten-percent WACC
and the Synopsys
enterprise-value cross-check.

I treated both
as calculations to test—
not conclusions to accept.

For WACC,
I independently checked
the four-point-seven-nine-percent
Treasury yield,

the four-point-two-three-percent
implied equity risk premium,

the industry beta,

Cadence's debt
and market-equity weights,

and the tax adjustment.

The calculation produced
ten-point-zero-one-one-five percent,
which I rounded to ten percent.

//

[SHOW ENTERPRISE-TO-EQUITY BRIDGE]

For the E-V cross-check,
I rebuilt Synopsys enterprise value
from market capitalization,
plus debt,
minus cash.

I divided that
by trailing-twelve-month revenue
and applied the
nine-point-one-two-four-nine-times multiple
to Cadence.

I then completed Cadence's separate
enterprise-to-equity bridge.

The model tests
confirmed the formulas,
and the visible output
preserved the result.

//

[SHOW AI-USE REGISTER]

I accepted those outputs
with qualifications.

The WACC uses
an industry beta
and a recent effective
debt-cost proxy.

Synopsys is only
one admitted peer,
and its Ansys acquisition
affects comparability.

AI helped accelerate
the calculation
and challenge the model,
but it did not make
my recommendation.

//

[CAMERA — FINAL RECONCILIATION]

The evidence was sufficient
because the accounting-linked D-C-F,
both peer checks,
the market-implied tests,
and the sensitivity work
all failed to support
three hundred thirteen dollars
and four cents.

My conclusion is not
that Cadence is a weak company.

It is that a strong company
can still be
an unattractive entry
at the wrong price.

That is why my final decision
is WATCH AND DEFER.
